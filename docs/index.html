Iran war stalls between talks and strikes

Two weeks of tanker attacks, a US blockade and shuttle diplomacy, with a possible US bombing campaign waiting on the far side of the Nov 3 midterms.

Bottom line up front

For the past two weeks the US–Iran war has sat in what Iran's leaders call "neither war nor peace." No US or Israeli strike on Iran was confirmed in this window. Fighting carried on at sea and on the Yemen–Saudi front. Iran kept attacking tankers around the Strait of Hormuz, and a US naval blockade held Iran's own oil exports at about zero.

Qatar is carrying a draft deal between Washington and Tehran, and an Iranian reply is expected around Oct 31. The core dispute is about order: the US wants Iran's nuclear program on the table first, and Iran wants the blockade and sanctions lifted first. Oil stays far above pre-war levels, with Brent near $116 on Oct 30 against $71 before the war.

Background

The war began on Feb 28, 2026, when US–Israeli strikes killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei; his son Mojtaba succeeded him. A spring ceasefire and a June peace memorandum both broke down, and fighting resumed on Jul 8 (Wikipedia). Since then the conflict has mostly been a contest of blockades: the US blocks Iran's ports, and Iran harasses shipping through Hormuz. A CENTCOM estimate puts the US cost at about $47 billion (CBS News). Counts of US military deaths range from 18 to 23 depending on the source (Al Jazeera).

Prices moved on headlines more than on barrels: talk of strikes pushed them up, and news of diplomacy pulled them down. On Oct 29 Brent fell about 1.7% after news of Vance's outreach (CNBC). Iran's seaborne exports are at zero under the blockade (Bloomberg), but overall Hormuz traffic has recovered to roughly 70–82% of pre-war levels, though estimates conflict (CNBC). War-risk insurance runs 6–10% of a tanker's value per trip, against about 0.25% before the war (Boston Report). Goldman Sachs estimates about $25/barrel of "war premium" in Brent and forecasts $90 by December (investingLive); UBS sees about $110.

For households, US diesel costs $6.35 a gallon (Al Jazeera) and US inflation rose to 3.7% in September (Inman). Gold, which usually rises in wartime, has fallen to about $3,900–4,000 from a January record near $5,600, as higher interest rates and a stronger dollar outweigh its safe-haven pull (investingLive).

Three things to watch

  1. Iran's answer to Qatar, around Oct 31

    CBS reports "signs of progress," and direct US–Iran talks could start as soon as next week in Qatar or Turkey (CBS News). The test is whether either side gives ground on which steps come first.

  2. What Washington does after Nov 3

    The reported plan for a "brief but intense" campaign has no final decision behind it, according to AP (The New Arab). The IAEA's inability to account for Iran's enriched uranium is the most likely stated reason if strikes resume.

  3. Attacks on shipping and Saudi oil sites

    More tanker strikes, or a Houthi hit on Saudi export routes, would land on a market with little cushion left. The US finished its 172-million-barrel reserve release on Oct 29 (DOE), and OPEC+ meets Nov 1 (Quartz).
