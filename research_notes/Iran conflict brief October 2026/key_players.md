# Key Players in the 2026 Iran War: Positions, Goals, Recent Actions, Red Lines (as of 30 Oct 2026)

Source note: Most October details come from live news (Reuters via syndication, AP via The New Arab live blog, Fox News live blog, Al Jazeera, CGTN, Israel Hayom, Tasnim/Xinhua). Wikipedia was used only for the background timeline. Several items come only from search-result summaries and are marked "(search snippet)". Treat those as less firmly verified. ## Q1: What is the current state of the conflict, and who are the principal actors that matter most right now?

### Takeaway
As of 30 Oct 2026 the US-Israel war on Iran, which began on 28 Feb 2026, is in a tense, partly frozen phase. There is no peace deal. The IRGC is keeping the Strait of Hormuz "closed" by mining it, the US keeps a counter-blockade on Iranian ports, and the Houthis are escalating against Saudi Arabia. Washington has told its partners to prepare for a possible renewed "brief but intense" campaign after the 3 Nov US midterms. The actors that matter most right now are the US (Trump), Iran's leadership and the IRGC, Qatar as lead mediator, and Israel, whose 27 Oct election ended in deadlock under a caretaker Netanyahu. Saudi Arabia and the Houthis form a second front.

### Cited Findings
**Background (pre-October context)**
- The war began on 28 Feb 2026 with US-Israeli strikes ("Operation Epic Fury"; Israel's part was "Roaring Lion"). The strikes killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and other officials. Iran answered with missile and drone attacks on Israel, US bases and Gulf states, and it closed Hormuz. — [Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war)
- Timeline per Wikipedia: Hezbollah-Israel fighting resumed around 2 March, and Israel occupied southern Lebanon. The UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Bahrain struck Iran in late March/April. A ceasefire began on 8 April. The Islamabad Talks failed on 12–13 April, and the US then imposed its own naval blockade. On 17 June the US and Iranian presidents signed a memorandum ending the war and lifting the "dual blockade". The ceasefire collapsed on 8 July after Iran attacked shipping in Hormuz. The Houthis opened a Bab al-Mandab crisis in September. — [Wikipedia: 2026 Iran war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war)
- Analysts and reporters now call the June MoU "now-defunct". — (search snippet referencing [JPost](http://fr.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-903180))

**Current military and economic picture (Oct 16–30)**
- 29–30 Oct: The IRGC said three tankers exploded after hitting mines on an "unauthorised southern route", and Iran's army reported a fourth. The IRGC says the strait is "closed" until Iran's demands are met. The US military said a day earlier that it had cleared key routes, and the US also keeps a counter-blockade on Iranian ports. — [The New Arab live blog](https://www.newarab.com/news/no-majority-israeli-election-trump-mulls-next-steps-iran)
- AP reported that the US has not struck Iran since early September. — (search snippet; AP original not fetched)
- The Pentagon told Congress the war had cost at least $47.1bn as of 16 Oct, up from a July estimate of $37.5bn. CENTCOM has more than 50,000 troops in the region. The Pentagon acknowledges 18 US service members killed; six officials told the Washington Post the real number is at least 22. — [Al Jazeera, 29 Oct](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/29/pentagon-says-iran-war-has-cost-us-taxpayers-47bn-what-that-means)
- Economic and political effects in the US: diesel at $6.35/gal against $3.75 a year earlier, Trump's approval at 33%, and Democrats ahead 51.4% to 43.2% in RCP's generic-ballot average, five days before the midterms. — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/29/pentagon-says-iran-war-has-cost-us-taxpayers-47bn-what-that-means)
- Casualty figures vary. Iran: 3,528 killed (Iranian figure), 3,684 (HRANA), 3,375+ (WHO). Israel: 41 service members and 31 civilians. US: 21–23 service members. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war). The US count conflicts with the Pentagon's acknowledged 18 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/29/pentagon-says-iran-war-has-cost-us-taxpayers-47bn-what-that-means)).

### Inferences
- The 3 Nov US midterms are the main near-term pivot. Mediators are visibly racing to land a framework before Trump decides on renewed strikes.
- Hormuz is Iran's central lever. The US port blockade and sanctions are Washington's. Each side is demanding that the other move first.

## Q2: What does each actor say it wants, what are analysts saying its real aims are, and what has it done recently (Oct 16–30)?

### Takeaway
The US wants the war "wrapped up soon", with guarantees that Iran never gets a nuclear weapon, and it keeps strikes on the table after the midterms. Iran is demanding sequencing: the US lifts its blockade and oil sanctions, unfreezes assets and agrees a region-wide ceasefire before Hormuz reopens and talks resume. It will not concede on nuclear issues up front. Israel's Netanyahu frames this as a "one-time opportunity" to defeat Iran, but he is a caretaker after an indecisive election. Gulf states, Turkey and Pakistan are pushing for a deal, while Saudi Arabia is under heavy Houthi fire. Russia and China give Iran political cover but want Hormuz open.

## Q3: Who is mediating, and what proposals are on the table?

### Takeaway
Qatar is the lead mediator, coordinated with Washington. Pakistan, Turkey, Saudi Arabia and Egypt support the effort. Three proposals are on the table: Qatar's draft peace plan, presented on 25 Oct and rejected by Iran as "unacceptable"; Iran's own 7-Day Plan; and a US back channel through Vance's letter to Rezaei. The gap is over sequencing. The US wants nuclear issues included first, while Iran wants the blockade and sanctions lifted first.

## Q4: Where do the nuclear program and sanctions fit in?

### Takeaway
The nuclear file is the core sticking point. The IAEA told the UNSC on 27 Oct that it cannot verify whether Iran has diverted its roughly 440 kg of 60%-enriched uranium, or where that material is. Iran refuses access to Isfahan, while the agency sees no sign of reconstruction at damaged sites. Sanctions relief, especially on oil, and unfrozen assets are Iran's main demands. The US is adding pressure, for example by freezing Iranian crypto assets.

### Cited Findings
- On 27 Oct Grossi told the UNSC the IAEA cannot verify non-diversion. He cited about 440 kg of high-enriched uranium in the previously declared stockpile, a pre-June 2025 estimate whose location is now unconfirmed. Iran has repeatedly refused access to the Isfahan Fuel Enrichment Plant, which it declared shortly before the June 2025 strikes.
